The reading room

What might remain without objective moral facts?

This collection centers on views that reject moral facts whose truth is completely independent of what people value or approve. It also includes nearby views about rules built through fair procedures, standards that vary across groups, and doubt about deserved punishment. The authors disagree with one another about moral language, judgment, blame, and what people should do next.

Moral Intuitions without a Moral Realm

Development, Reification, and the Affective Genealogy of Value

Some actions do not merely bother us. They can seem wrong in themselves. This paper asks whether that force is evidence of a moral fact, or whether it is the finished result of emotion, learning, social rules, moral language, reflection, and habit.

Its strongest conclusion is deliberately limited: learning how an intuition was formed can weaken its automatic authority as evidence for a non-natural moral realm. That history does not, by itself, prove every moral claim false or settle every form of moral realism.
