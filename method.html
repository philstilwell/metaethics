How the diagnostics work

Transparent questions. Limited conclusions.

Every lab is designed to show exactly which answer produced which result. None can prove that a moral theory is true, read a hidden belief, or measure a whole person.

Shared design rules

Six promises every lab should keep

If a lab cannot keep one of these promises, its result should say so rather than hide the problem.

  1. 01

    One question at a time

    Meaning, truth, motivation, authority, usefulness, agreement, and emotional force are kept separate unless a question deliberately compares them.

  2. 02

    Fixed case details

    Each scenario states what to assume and what the question asks. Users are not scored for inventing missing facts.

  3. 03

    Visible scoring rules

    Each answer says which result category it supports. No hidden model or AI interprets the response.

  4. 04

    Clear bar labels

    A bar means “times selected out of times offered.” It is not a probability, grade, personality score, or chance that a theory is true.

  5. 05

    Specific points to compare

    A flag identifies two exact answers that may need more explanation. It is not automatically a contradiction or proof that the user is irrational.

  6. 06

    Local privacy

    Answers remain in the open page. Saving a map summary is optional, device-only, erasable, and excludes full answers and AI prompts.

How to read a result

Four labels that do less than they may sound like

Pattern

An answer category that appears several times in these cases. It is not a permanent identity or complete philosophy.

Skill check

A question with a defined best answer, such as telling a cause apart from evidence. It does not grade the user’s moral character.

Point to compare

Two answers that may use different sources, cover different cases, or lead to different results. They need explanation, but they are not yet a contradiction.

Result under an assumption

A conclusion that follows only while using the starting assumption stated by the lab. Rejecting that assumption may change the result.

What each lab can and cannot show

01 · Choice

Moral Profile

Shows
Which offered reasons you select across six decisions and two comparisons.
Does not show
Your complete moral theory, character, truth, or consistency across every possible case.
Compares only
Choices that appear to conflict with the reason you selected, plus one later rule about personal relationships.

02 · Meaning

What Do You Mean by Wrong?

Shows
Which of six plain-language meanings you give moral sentences in seven settings.
Does not show
Whether the claim is true or whether that way of using moral language is best for society.
Compares only
Four stated differences among the meanings, plus any change between your opening and final answers.

Quality control

The code checks the claims the prose makes.

Automated checks confirm the number of questions, possible result categories, bar calculations, ties, answer explanations, AI-prompt context, links, and rules for flagging answers. When possible, the checks cover every answer path. Passing these checks shows that the site follows its stated rules. It does not show that the philosophical categories are complete or true.
