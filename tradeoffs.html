Does “cannot be put on one scale” mean “anything goes”?

No. A comparison can protect firm limits, give some needs priority, consider several different reasons, or use a fair process. The explanation must say what guides the choice and who must live with it.

The main lesson

A hard comparison needs a stated rule, not a hidden price for every value.

Adding up effects, protecting firm limits, using emergency cutoffs, setting priorities, considering several reasons, using fair procedures, and choosing workable settlements do different jobs. A complete view says which method comes first, when another method takes over, and what loss remains even after a choice is made.
